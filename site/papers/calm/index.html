CALM

Continuous Audio Language Models

Rouard*, Orsini*, Roebel, Zeghidour, Défossez

Replace RVQ tokens with continuous VAE frames. A causal Transformer reads the past frames and outputs one vector; a small MLP trained as a consistency model turns random noise into the next frame in 1 to 4 calls. On speech and music this matches or beats RVQ language models, with a far cheaper per-frame sampler.

Motivation

RVQ tokens of a frame depend on each other

An RVQ codec writes each audio frame as a stack of tokens. Each level encodes what the previous levels missed, so a language model has to generate a frame's tokens one after another (the RQ-Transformer), and better audio means more levels.

CALM keeps the encoder's vector as it is and generates all of it in one pass. It builds on MAR (image generation), where a small diffusion MLP samples each continuous token. CALM found that setup slow and unstable on long audio.

1Tokenizer

A VAE instead of an RVQ codec

The audio tokenizer keeps the Mimi recipe (causal convolutions plus Transformers, adversarial training) and swaps the RVQ bottleneck for a VAE bottleneck with a Gaussian prior. Following Evans et al. (2024).

Objective (eq. 2)
LVAE = λtLt(x, x̂) + λfLf(x, x̂) + λadvLadv(x̂) + λfeatLfeat(x, x̂) + λKLLKL + λdistillLdistill
In words

The decoded audio x̂ must match the input x in time and frequency and fool discriminators. The latents must stay close to a standard Gaussian. For speech, the whole latent is also pulled toward WavLM features, so it carries phonetic content.

QIs the VAE trained separately from the backbone?
Yes. It is trained first, with its own loss. The stage-2 loss (eq. 3) trains the backbone, the short-context Transformer and the head; the VAE is not among those parameters, and the paper frames CALM, like MAR, as modelling the latents of a pretrained VAE. Joint training is not tried.
2Language model

Next-frame prediction with a noisy long context and a clean short one

This is an ordinary autoregressive language model with frames in place of tokens. To produce frame s, a causal Transformer reads frames 1 to s−1 and outputs one vector Zs. The head (§3) turns Zs into a sample of frame s, which is appended, and the loop goes on. Training uses teacher forcing, as with tokens.

On music, this plain setup drifts: small errors in generated frames accumulate and the audio degrades within seconds. CALM adds two pieces, shown below.

Noise augmentation (music)
x̃s = √ks · εs + √(1 − ks) · xs,   ks ~ U(0, 1),  εs ~ 𝒩(0, I)
In words

During training, every frame fed to the long-context Transformer gets its own random amount of noise, from none to pure noise. At generation it reads its own imperfect frames without noise, and it has learned not to trust any single frame too much. Taken from Pasini et al. (2024b), with square roots so the variance stays the same.

Conditioning
Zs = Tlong(x̃1, …, x̃s−1) + Tshort(xs−K, …, xs−1)
In words

A large causal Transformer reads the whole noised past for coarse structure. A small causal Transformer reads only the last K = 10 clean frames for fine detail. Their outputs are summed and condition the head.

With noise alone, music kept its rhythm but lost its instruments and faded to silence after 10–15 s: the noise keeps fine detail out of the long context. The clean short context brings that detail back to the head. Speech needs neither piece; the authors saw no gain there. Ablation on music (FAD, lower is better, Tab. 5):

Head batch multiplier. The backbone dominates the cost of a training step, so each Zs is reused for 8 head losses with different noise draws. Training converges faster.

Speech continuation: inner monologue
Taken from Moshi. The backbone also reads and predicts a time-aligned transcript of its own speech; the same output vector feeds a text classifier and the head. Audio lags the text by 2 frames (160 ms), so the next word is known before its sound is produced.
3Consistency head I got stuck here

Turning noise into the next frame in one call

Many next frames are plausible, so the head must draw one of them at random rather than output their average, which would sound blurred. With tokens, the softmax gives the distribution. Here, a small MLP turns random noise into a frame, conditioned on Zs.

Background: flow matching

Link every training frame to a noise sample by a path that blends one into the other. A flow-matching network learns which way to move at any point on such a path. To sample, you start from noise and follow those directions back to data in small steps, one network call per step. The directions change along the way, so a few large steps land in the wrong place.

Noising path (TrigFlow, Lu & Song 2025)
xt = cos(t) · x0 + sin(t) · ε,   t ∈ [0, π/2]
In words

At t = 0 the point is the clean frame, at t = π/2 it is pure noise.

Flow matching loss
LFM = 𝔼 ‖ Fφ(xt, t) − dxt/dt ‖²
In words

Predict the direction of motion along the path from the noisy point and its noise level. CALM's TrigFlow head is this model, sampled in many steps.

Consistency: learn where the path ends

A consistency model skips the walk. It learns a function f that takes any point on a path, with its noise level, and returns where that path ends. Sampling is one call: draw noise, apply f, get a frame.

The hard part is training, since nobody knows where the path through a noisy point ends. Two facts make it learnable. At noise level 0 the answer is known: a clean point is already the end of its path, so f returns its input, and this is built into the network. And two nearby points on the same path have the same end, so f must give them the same answer. Training enforces this agreement, and correct answers spread from the clean end of each path toward the noisy end.

Parametrization
fφ(xt, t, Z) = cskip(t) · xt + cout(t) · Fφ(xt, t, Z),   cskip(0) = 1, cout(0) = 0
In words

At noise level 0 the output is exactly the input. This is the anchor from the paragraph above.

CALM objective (eq. 3), continuous-time consistency (Lu & Song 2025)
L = Σs 𝔼t, ε [ ewψ(t)⁄D ‖ Fφ(xts, t, Zs) − Fφ̄(xts, t, Zs) − cos(t) · dfφ̄(xts, t, Zs)⁄dt ‖² − wψ(t) ],  xts = cos(t) xs + sin(t) ε
In words

Noise the true frame xs to a random level t. The loss is zero when the predicted end stops changing as t moves along the path (df/dt = 0): the agreement rule in continuous form. The target is built from the network's own output with gradients stopped (φ̄); wψ is a learned weight per noise level. Gradients also reach the backbone through Zs, so this one loss trains the whole language model.

Sampling
x̂s = fφ(ε, π/2, Zs),   ε ~ 𝒩(0, I)
In words

One call on pure noise gives the next frame. With 4 steps (music), the estimate is re-noised to a lower level and the head is called again; a trained f is only approximate, and these extra calls correct it.

Alternative head used for TTS: Lagrangian Self-Distillation
From Boffi et al. (2025). The network learns a jump between any two noise levels instead of only to level 0, trained with a mix of flow matching and self-distillation. For the ~300M text-to-speech model, 1-step LSD clearly beat 1-step consistency (MUSHRA 61.1 vs 46.8), and Pocket TTS uses it.
4Sampling controls

Temperature and guidance without logits

A token model controls sampling through its softmax. The consistency head has none, so each control moves to the input noise or to Zs.

Gaussian temperature (Sec. 4.5)
ε ~ 𝒩(0, τ I)
In words

Shrinking the input noise is exactly a temperature on the Gaussian: samples stay in its likely region, trading diversity for quality. τ = 0.8 works for speech continuation, as for the RVQ model.

Latent classifier-free guidance (Sec. 4.6)
ZCFG = Z∅ + α (ZC − Z∅)
In words

Compute Z with and without the condition (text, CLAP embedding), push past the conditioned one, and give the result to the head. From SoundReactor (Saito et al., 2025).

Latent distillation (Sec. 4.7)
‖ Zstudent − ZCFG(teacher) ‖²
In words

A student backbone learns to output the guided Z directly, so guidance costs one pass instead of two. The head is copied unchanged and the student can be smaller: Pocket TTS goes from 24 to 6 layers, 100M parameters with the VAE decoder, faster than real time on a laptop CPU.

Results

Better than RVQ at a fraction of the sampling cost

Speech continuation. The 1-step head beats the 8-level RVQ model on perplexity, acoustic quality and human-rated meaningfulness. Its sampler is 12.3× faster, but the whole generation only 1.3×, because the 2.2B backbone dominates.

Text-to-speech. With a 1-step LSD head, CALM has the lowest word error rate among the compared systems (1.81).

Limitations

Open points

  • Quality vs speed. The best music FAD (0.64) needs the 100-step TrigFlow head, which is not real time.
  • The head depends on scale. At ~300M, 1-step consistency was clearly worse than 1-step LSD.
  • Music VAE. It needs at least 96 dimensions to match a 32-level RVQ codec, and has no semantic distillation yet.
