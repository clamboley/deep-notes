> [!abstract] TL;DR
> CALM replaces the RVQ codec + RQ-Transformer head with a VAE bottleneck + single-step consistency MLP, generating continuous latents autoregressively. On both speech and music it beats the discrete RQ-Transformer baseline in quality while running the sampler 12–19× faster, making high-fidelity streaming generation practical without quantization loss.

## Context

The dominant audio LM stack is RVQ-GAN codec (EnCodec / SoundStream / Mimi) + autoregressive Transformer. A Residual Vector Quantizer maps each audio frame into a coarse-to-fine hierarchy of $K$ discrete tokens. Three families of discrete AR models deal with the depth axis differently:

- **Flat** (AudioLM, Borsos 2023): flatten the $K \times S$ token matrix — sequence length ×$K$, prohibitively expensive at high $K$.
- **Delay pattern** (MusicGen, Copet 2023): generate all $K$ tokens in parallel with a $K-1$-frame shift — enables batched sampling but introduces 160ms latency per RVQ level.
- **RQ-Transformer** (Lee 2022, Moshi 2024): a second Transformer head decodes the $K$ codebook levels sequentially at each timestep — low latency but compute scales with $K$.

CALM attacks the root cause: because RVQ is lossy, high fidelity requires deep hierarchies, and any approach that processes the depth axis sees its cost grow with quality. The continuous alternative — model VAE latents directly — eliminates the depth axis. This builds on MAR (Li et al. 2024) for images, which replaced VQ with a diffusion head over continuous tokens. CALM's contribution is making this fast enough (consistency instead of diffusion) and stable enough (noise injection + short-context transformer) to work on rich audio.

## Core Idea

Instead of quantizing audio frames into discrete codes, encode each frame into a continuous latent $\mathbf{x}^s \in \mathbb{R}^C$ with a VAE (Gaussian KL prior). Run a causal Transformer over the latent sequence to produce a conditioning vector, then sample the next continuous latent with a one-step consistency MLP. No RVQ hierarchy → no depth axis → no RQ-Transformer.

The quality-compute trade: discrete models pay for fidelity in **bitrate** (more RVQ levels → more sequence and depth → more compute), with an irreducible quantization floor. Continuous models pay for fidelity in **sampler steps** (more denoising steps → better quality), then use consistency distillation to collapse that cost toward one.

## Architecture

Three components, trained jointly end-to-end.

### VAE-GAN bottleneck

Replaces the RVQ bottleneck in a standard RVQ-GAN. Architecture follows Mimi (Défossez et al. 2024): fully causal, convolutions + Transformer layers in encoder and decoder, KL regularization enforcing a Gaussian prior.

**Music VAE:** 32 kHz, 25 Hz frame rate, 128 latent dims. Needs ≥96 dims to match 32-RVQ EnCodec on ViSQOL/SISNR. At 128 dims: ViSQOL 4.01, SISNR 10.3.  
**Speech VAE:** 24 kHz, 12.5 Hz frame rate, 32 latent dims. WavLM semantic distillation applied across all latent dims (vs. Mimi which applies only to the first codebook). Matches 8-RVQ Mimi on MOSNet (3.15 vs. 3.11) and beats it on ABX (8.1% vs. 9.4%).

VAE loss:
$$\mathcal{L}_\text{VAE} = \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_t(x,\hat{x}) + \lambda_f \mathcal{L}_f(x,\hat{x}) + \lambda_\text{adv}\mathcal{L}_\text{adv}(\hat{x}) + \lambda_\text{feat}\mathcal{L}_\text{feat}(x,\hat{x}) + \lambda_\text{KL}\mathcal{L}_\text{KL}$$

Reconstruction losses $\mathcal{L}_t$ and $\mathcal{L}_f$ are used for music but dropped for speech (using adversarial + KL + distillation alone).

### Long-context backbone with noise injection

A causal Transformer $T_{\text{long},\theta^1}$ consumes the full history. **During training**, input latents are noised before entering the backbone:

$$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^s = \sqrt{k_s}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon_s + \sqrt{1-k_s}\,\mathbf{x}^s, \qquad k_s \sim \mathcal{U}(0,1),\quad \boldsymbol\epsilon_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$$

The $\sqrt{\cdot}$ scaling preserves variance on the Gaussian inputs. This is the fix for autoregressive drift: without it, rollout diverges as the backbone feeds on its own increasingly imperfect outputs. **At inference**, noise is dropped entirely.

The tradeoff: forcing the backbone to see degraded inputs makes it represent coarse, robust long-term features — but suppresses the fine-grained local structure the consistency head also needs.

**Sizes:** music 1.35B (dim 1536, 24 heads, 48 layers); speech 2.2B (dim 2560, 20 heads, 24 layers).

### Short-context transformer

A lightweight causal Transformer $T_{\text{short},\theta^2}$ attends to the $K=10$ most recent *clean* latents (≈0.4s at 25 Hz):

$$\mathbf{z}^s_\text{short} = T_{\text{short},\theta^2}(\mathbf{x}^{s-K},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{s-1})$$

It provides the fine-grained local signal the noise injection strips from the backbone. Music uses it; speech does not (inner monologue provides equivalent phonetic grounding). The ablation: removing it sends FAD from 0.93 → 4.03 — the single most important component.

**Size:** music only — 113M (dim 1536, 24 heads, 4 layers, $K=10$).

### Consistency MLP head

A small MLP $f_\phi$ conditioned on $\mathbf{Z}^s = \mathbf{z}^s_\text{long} + \mathbf{z}^s_\text{short}$ samples the next latent in one step.

**Music:** 601M (12 layers, MLP dim 3072, SiLU). **Speech:** 10M (6 layers, MLP dim 512, SiLU).

For reference, the RQ-Transformer head this replaces is 701M for both speech and music.

## Consistency Modeling

CALM uses the TrigFlow (Lu & Song 2025) continuous-time formulation. The noising path is:

$$\mathbf{x}_t = \cos(t)\,\mathbf{x}_0 + \sin(t)\,\boldsymbol\epsilon, \qquad t \in \left[0, \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right], \quad \boldsymbol\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$$

$\alpha_t = \cos(t)$, $\sigma_t = \sin(t)$ preserves total variance ($\cos^2 + \sin^2 = 1$). At $t=0$: clean data. At $t=\pi/2$: pure noise.

**Flow matching** trains $F_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ to predict the velocity along this path; generation integrates the probability-flow ODE backward from noise. Because the trajectory is curved, a single Euler step from pure noise fails — hence hundreds of steps for diffusion/flow heads.

**Consistency model** $f_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$ directly maps any noisy $\mathbf{x}_t$ to the corresponding clean $\mathbf{x}_0$ in one evaluation. Parameterized as:

$$f_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t, t) = c_\text{skip}(t)\,\mathbf{x}_t + c_\text{out}(t)\,F_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t, t)$$

with $c_\text{skip}(0)=1$, $c_\text{out}(0)=0$ to enforce the boundary condition $f_\phi(\mathbf{x}, 0) = \mathbf{x}$. The network $F_\phi$ only learns the correction away from the identity.

The continuous-time training loss (Lu & Song 2025) avoids the discrete timestep pairs of Song et al. 2023. Taking $\Delta t \to 0$ turns the "two outputs must agree" consistency condition into a differential one — the output must be instantaneously invariant along the trajectory:

$$\mathcal{L}_\text{CM}(\phi,\psi) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}_t,t}\!\left[\frac{e^{w_\psi(t)}}{D}\Big\|F_\phi(\mathbf{x}_t,t) - F_{\phi^-}(\mathbf{x}_t,t) - \cos(t)\frac{df_{\phi^-}(\mathbf{x}_t,t)}{dt}\Big\|_2^2 - w_\psi(t)\right]$$

Three load-bearing pieces:

- **$\frac{df_{\phi^-}}{dt}$ tangent term**: replaces a discrete timestep pair with a Jacobian-vector product, encoding the infinitesimal consistency constraint. The $\cos(t)$ factor falls out of the TrigFlow parameterization directly.
- **Stop-gradient teacher $\phi^-$**: no EMA schedule — the teacher is just the current network with gradients detached, which eliminates the training instabilities of earlier consistency model formulations.
- **Learned adaptive weight $w_\psi(t)$**: the $e^{w_\psi(t)}\cdot(\text{loss}) - w_\psi(t)$ form prevents any single noise level from dominating the gradient.

## Full Training Objective

All parameters — $T_{\text{long},\theta^1}$, $T_{\text{short},\theta^2}$, $f_\phi$, $w_\psi$ — are trained jointly with a single loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_\text{CALM}(\theta,\phi,\psi) = \sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathbb{E}_{t,\boldsymbol\epsilon}\!\left[\frac{e^{w_\psi(t)}}{D}\Big\|F_\phi(\mathbf{x}^s_t,t,\mathbf{Z}^s) - F_{\bar\phi}(\mathbf{x}^s_t,t,\mathbf{Z}^s) - \cos(t)\frac{df_{\bar\phi}(\mathbf{x}^s_t,t,\mathbf{Z}^s)}{dt}\Big\|_2^2 - w_\psi(t)\right]$$

where $\mathbf{Z}^s = \mathbf{z}^s_\text{long} + \mathbf{z}^s_\text{short}$, $t \sim [0,1]$, $\mathbf{x}^s_t = \cos(t)\mathbf{x}^s + \sin(t)\boldsymbol\epsilon$. Directly analogous to training a discrete backbone + RQ-Transformer under cross-entropy — the continuous consistency loss replaces the classification objective.

## Practical Additions

### Head batch multiplier

The expensive computation per step is $\mathbf{z}^s_\text{long}$ from the large backbone. Rather than draw one $(t, \boldsymbol\epsilon)$ sample per frame, compute $\mathbf{z}^s_\text{long}$ once and reuse it across $N=8$ independently sampled noise levels and noise vectors. Amortizes backbone cost and averages the noisy consistency gradient. FAD degrades from 0.93 → 1.32 without it; Fig. 2 shows substantially faster convergence with $N=8$.

![[calm-fig2.png]]
*Figure 2: FAD over training time for music CALM with head batch multipliers of 2, 4, and 8. A multiplier of 8 converges to FAD ≈0.71 in ~3 days; multiplier 2 is still above 2.0 at that point — the same backbone cost buys ~3× better quality by reusing $\mathbf{z}^s_\text{long}$.*

### Gaussian temperature sampling

Temperature is critical for speech naturalness and diversity. Categorical heads get it by sharpening/flattening the softmax. For the consistency head, the only stochastic input is Gaussian $\boldsymbol\epsilon$. The proper temperature change-of-variables requires the Jacobian $\partial f/\partial\boldsymbol\epsilon$, which is intractable. Approximation: sample $\boldsymbol\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^{-1}\mathbf{I})$ instead of $\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I})$. Fig. 3 confirms this empirically tracks the diversity-vs-temperature curve of the discrete model.

![[calm-fig3.png]]
*Figure 3: Average pairwise WavLM speaker similarity vs. sampling temperature for CALM and the RQ-Transformer over 100 unprompted speech generations. Both show the same decreasing trend — lower temperature concentrates speaker identity, higher temperature diversifies — confirming the Gaussian variance heuristic approximates discrete temperature control.*

## Results

On speech (27s from a 3s prompt), the 1-step consistency CALM at τ=0.8 ranks first on every automatic and human metric against the 8-RVQ RQ-Transformer baseline — better PPX, VERT, acoustic quality MOS, and meaningfulness Elo — while running the sampler 12.3× faster (2.9% vs. 26.7% of total inference time) for a 1.3× overall speedup. On music (30s from 3s), the 4-step consistency CALM beats the 32-RVQ RQ-Transformer on FAD (0.71 vs. 1.06) and acoustic quality at 1.9× overall speedup and 19.3× sampler speedup; the RQ-Transformer head had been consuming 57.7% of inference time, CALM's takes 6.6–20.1%. TrigFlow reaches the best absolute FAD (0.64) but only at 100 steps (RTF=0.3) — consistency specifically wins the RTF≥1 streamable regime (≤10 steps), while TrigFlow dominates at ≥25 steps. The ablation identifies the short-context transformer as the most critical component (FAD 0.93 → 4.03 without it), followed by noise augmentation (→1.63) and the head batch multiplier (→1.32); removing all three (≈MAR) gives FAD 8.38. Scaling to a 3B backbone brings CALM to FAD=0.62 vs. 0.98 for the equivalent 3B RQ-Transformer.

## Speech-Specific Architecture

Speech CALM is structurally simpler: no short-context transformer, no noise injection into the backbone, 10M head. The key difference is the **inner monologue**: a latent text stream aligned word-by-word with the audio, prepended 2 frames (160ms) ahead of the corresponding acoustic tokens. At each timestep $s$, the backbone takes both text tokens and audio latents as input and produces text logits alongside the audio conditioning $\mathbf{z}^s_\text{long}$.

Inner monologue pins down the next phoneme in advance, making the per-frame conditional $p(\mathbf{x}^s | \mathbf{Z}^s)$ nearly deterministic — hence a 10M head suffices vs. 601M for music. The backbone fine-tunes from Helium-1 (Kyutai 2025), a pretrained 2B multilingual LM, trained 150K steps on French+English speech.
