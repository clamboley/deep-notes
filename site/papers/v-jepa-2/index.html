V-JEPA 2

Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning

Assran, Bardes, Fan, Garrido, Howes, Komeili, Muckley, … LeCun, Rabbat, Ballas

Pretrain a video encoder by predicting the features of masked spacetime regions (1M+ hours of video, no actions, no pixel reconstruction). Freeze it. Train a small action-conditioned predictor on 62 h of robot video. Control a robot arm by searching for the actions whose predicted embedding lands closest to the embedding of a goal image.

observed: encoded from real frames predicted by a model target or goal
DataVideo + imagesVideoMix22M: 22M samples, 1M+ hours
Stage 1V-JEPA 2Mask denoising in feature space. ViT-g encoder, 1B params
Stage 2 · encoder frozenV-JEPA 2-ACNew 300M predictor: next-frame embedding given action and arm state. 62 h of Droid
Stage 3 · test timePlanningCEM over actions toward a goal image, re-planned every step. Franka arms, zero-shot
Same frozen encoder also feeds attentive probes (SSv2, EK100) and an LLM for video QA

Keep in mind

  • Everything lives in the frozen encoder's embedding space: training targets, predictions, goals. Nothing is decoded to pixels.
  • Actions only appear in stage 2, and they come for free: an action is the change in end-effector state between two frames.
  • The world model is never trained to act. Acting is test-time optimization: minimize the distance between the imagined embedding and the goal embedding.
  • In the experiments the planning horizon is 1 and the plan is redone after every move, so control is greedy. Pick-and-place needs hand-given sub-goal images.
  • 16 s per action versus 4 min for the pixel-space Cosmos world model, with higher success on every task.
1Pretraining · action-free · self-supervised

Predict masked video in feature space

The clip is cut into tokens, most of them are hidden, and the model must predict the representations of the hidden tokens from the visible ones.

Objective (eq. 1)
minθ, φ, Δy  ‖ Pφ(Δy, Eθ(x)) − sg(Eθ̄(y)) ‖1
In words

Given what the encoder made of the visible tokens, plus a placeholder for each hidden position, the predictor must output the features that the EMA encoder computes for those positions when it sees the whole clip. L1 distance, on masked positions only. x = masked clip, y = full clip, sg = stop-gradient.

Features, not pixels

Pixel targets force the model to spend capacity on unpredictable detail (texture, noise). Feature targets let it keep what is predictable: objects, positions, motion.

No collapse

If both sides were trained freely, mapping everything to a constant would minimize the loss. Stop-gradient and a slow EMA target prevent that.

Cheap and hard

Masked tokens are dropped, so the 1B encoder processes only the ~10% that is visible, and inferring the rest requires real spatiotemporal understanding.

What scaling bought

Training schedule and resolution
Warmup (12K it) and a long constant-LR phase (228K it) run on short, low-resolution clips: 16 frames at 256². Only the final cooldown (12K it, LR decayed) moves to longer, sharper clips, up to 64 frames at 384². This gives a model that handles 64×384² inputs at 8.4× less GPU time than training at that resolution throughout. Position encoding is 3D-RoPE (feature dims split across time, height, width), which they found stabilizes the largest models.
2Post-training · encoder frozen

V-JEPA 2-AC: an action-conditioned predictor

Stage 2 keeps the stage-1 encoder, frozen, and works entirely on its embeddings. A new predictor learns to map past embeddings, actions and arm states to the next frame's embedding, i.e. the dynamics of the world inside the stage-1 representation space.

data
Droid: teleoperated Franka Panda with a 2-finger gripper. Clips ≥ 4 s only, leaving under 62 h (23k trajectories, successes and failures). No reward, task label or success flag is used.
clip
4 s at 4 fps → 16 frames at 256×256.
state sk
7-D end-effector state: position (3), Euler orientation (3), gripper (1).
action ak
sk+1 − sk, the change in state between consecutive frames. No action labels needed.
frame zk
E(xk), each frame encoded on its own by the frozen ViT-g → a 16×16×1408 feature map.
predictor
~300M transformer: 24 layers, 16 heads, width 1024. Linear projections in for actions, states and patches; linear projection out to 1408.
Teacher forcing (eq. 2), T = 15
Ltf = 1⁄T Σk ‖ Pφ((at, st, E(xt))t≤k) − E(xk+1) ‖1

Predict each next embedding from the true past. All 15 predictions come out of one forward pass (the causal mask makes them independent of the future), then are averaged into one loss.

Rollout (eq. 3), T = 2
Lrollout = ‖ Pφ(a1:T; s1, z1) − zT+1 ‖1

Start from the true first frame, feed the model's own prediction back in, compare only the end point. With T = 2 the gradient goes through one recurrent step. This trains the regime planning uses: rolling forward on its own outputs.

Total (eq. 4)
L(φ) = Ltf + Lrollout

Only the predictor weights φ are trained.

3Test time · no training I got stuck here

Inferring actions by planning

Every weight is frozen. The actions become the variables of an optimization: find the actions whose imagined outcome, in embedding space, is closest to the embedding of a goal image.

Energy (eq. 5)
ℰ(â1:T; zk, sk, zg) = ‖ P(â1:T; sk, zk) − zg ‖1
a⋆1:T = argminâ ℰ
In words

Encode the current frame and the goal image with the same frozen encoder. For a candidate action sequence, roll the predictor forward T steps from the current state. The energy is the L1 distance between that imagined embedding and the goal embedding. Low energy means "doing this would make the scene look like the goal".

Minimizing it: the Cross-Entropy Method

Gradient-free. Keep a Gaussian per action coordinate, sample candidates, score each one by running the predictor, refit the Gaussian to the best few, repeat. Return the final mean.

800samples per CEM iteration
10CEM iterations per decision
1planning horizon T in the robot runs
16 sper action on one RTX 4090

Receding horizon: plan, do one step, look again

QDid they try sampling a whole trajectory and executing it without re-planning?
Not in the main text: every robot result uses closed-loop MPC. With horizon 1, a planning call yields a single action, so open-loop execution would first require long-horizon planning, which the paper flags as hard: autoregressive errors grow with rollout length, and the search space grows exponentially with the horizon. Re-planning from a real observation each step is what makes a short-horizon, imperfect model usable. The appendices weren't checked for such an ablation.

Sub-goals for pick-and-place

Grasp and reach-with-object use one goal image. Pick-and-place is too long for greedy planning, so three goal images are given in sequence, each pursued for a fixed number of steps.

Results

Zero-shot on Franka arms in two new labs

Neither lab appears in Droid. Uncalibrated monocular RGB camera, no data collected in these environments, no task-specific training or reward.

The frozen encoder elsewhere

Understanding77.3

SSv2 top-1 with an attentive probe (ViT-g, 384²). Best 6-task average (88.2). Strong on motion; on appearance it trails image-text encoders slightly (ImageNet 85.1 vs 88.0 for SigLIP2).

Anticipation39.7

EPIC-Kitchens-100 action recall@5, 1 s ahead: +44% relative over the previous best (PlausiVL, 8B). The pretraining predictor fills in the future frame via mask tokens; a probe reads encoder + predictor outputs.

Video QA84.0

PerceptionTest with Llama 3.1 8B. State of the art at 8B on MVP, TempCompass, TemporalBench, TOMATO. With a frozen encoder it beats DINOv2, SigLIP2 and PE, so no language supervision is needed in the encoder.

Limitations

Where it breaks

  • Camera position. Actions are in the robot-base frame, but the model must infer those axes from one uncalibrated view, often without the base visible. They tried several camera placements by hand before picking one.
  • Long horizons. Rollout error grows with each autoregressive step and the action search space grows exponentially with the horizon. Hence horizon 1 and hand-given sub-goals.
  • Image goals only. A goal must be a picture of the end state. Language goals, possibly via the LLM-aligned encoder, are left to future work.
  • Precision. Grasping the box succeeds 25% of the time on average; small action errors miss the cup rim or leave the gripper too narrow.
